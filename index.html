Neehal Tumma

Hi, I'm Neehal! I am a second-year PhD student at MIT CSAIL, advised by Prof. Daniela Rus. I also work at Liquid AI on scaling efficient foundation models.

My research interests broadly lie in architecture design for sequence modeling, particularly LLMs. Within this space, I primarily focus on recurrent architectures as alternatives to attention-based ones. I am interested in exploring these recurrent primitives in various settings, including test-time training, continual learning, and efficient long-context modeling.

I graduated from Harvard in 2024 with an SM in Computer Science and an AB in Mathematics and Computer Science. During my undergrad, I was fortunate enough to work with Prof. Nir Shavit and Prof. Finale Doshi-Velez.

Publications
ParaTTT: Parallelizing Test-Time Training Layers with Deep Memories
Noel Loo*, Neehal Tumma*, T. Konstantin Rusch, Mathias Lechner, Alexander Amini, Daniela Rus
Preprint, 2026
paper / code

We introduce ParaTTT, an algorithm for parallelizing test-time training layers with deep nonlinear memories through iterative refinement. The exact online recurrence is a fixed point of the iteration. With two refinement steps, ParaTTT trains 4.9× faster than TTT-MLP at 340M scale while improving recall over linear recurrent models.

Quantizing Recurrent Language Models Is Easier Than You Think
Philipp Nazari*, Neehal Tumma*, Daniela Rus, T. Konstantin Rusch
Preprint, 2026
paper / code

We show that decayed recurrent models develop weaker residual outliers than softmax Transformers, making their weights and activations easier to quantize. For the recurrent state, we introduce LoReS, combining scale grouping, decay-based mixed precision, and stochastic rounding to support robust 8-bit state quantization and faster decoding in vLLM.

LinQuant: Functional Invariances for Low-Precision Linear Recurrences
Neehal Tumma*, Philipp Nazari*, Rohan Cherukuri, Daniela Rus, T. Konstantin Rusch
Preprint, 2026
paper / code / tweet

We identify seven functional invariances that preserve the full-precision computation of linear recurrences while making their operands easier to quantize. LinQuant uses these transformations to improve low-precision prefill across recurrent architectures, improving perplexity at matched latency on Mamba-2 and Nemotron-H.

Preconditioned DeltaNet: Curvature-aware Sequence Modeling for Linear Recurrences
Neehal Tumma*, Noel Loo*, Daniela Rus
ICML, 2026
paper / code / tweet

We show that with least-squares preconditioning, DeltaNet and linear attention reduce to the same recurrence. Using this insight, we introduce preconditioned variants of DeltaNet (PDN), Gated DeltaNet (PGDN), and Kimi Delta Attention (PKDA), demonstrating gains across synthetic recall benchmarks and language modeling at the 340M and 1B scale.

LFM2 Technical Report
Liquid AI Team
arXiv, 2025
paper / tweet

We introduce LFM2, a family of compact foundation models (350M–8.3B parameters) optimized for edge deployment. Using hardware-in-the-loop architecture search, LFM2 achieves up to 2x faster prefill and decode on CPUs compared to similarly sized models, with extensions to vision-language, speech, and retrieval.

Quantifying Memory Utilization with Effective State-Size
Rom N. Parnichkun*, Neehal Tumma*, Armin W. Thomas, Alessandro Moro, Qi An, Taiji Suzuki, Atsushi Yamashita, Michael Poli, Stefano Massaroli
ICML, 2025
paper / tweet

We introduce effective state-size (ESS), a principled metric grounded in signal processing and control theory for quantifying how sequence models utilize memory. ESS provides interpretable insights into memory dynamics across attention, convolutions, and recurrent architectures, enabling improved initialization, regularization, and distillation.

A connectomics-driven analysis reveals novel characterization of border regions in mouse visual cortex
Neehal Tumma*, Linghao Kong*, Shashata Sawmya, Tony Tong Wang, Nir Shavit
Neural Networks, 2025
paper

We propose a statistical sliding window framework for analyzing neural connectivity in large-scale connectomics datasets using both the connectivity graph and functional activations. Applied to the MICrONS dataset, we find that the V1-RL border region exhibits greater synaptic connectivity and more synchronous activity, acting as a bridge between visual areas.

Leveraging Low-Rank and Sparse Recurrent Connectivity for Robust Closed-Loop Control
Neehal Tumma, Mathias Lechner, Noel Loo, Ramin Hasani, Daniela Rus
ICLR, 2024   (Spotlight, top 5% of submissions)
paper / tweet

We explore how the recurrent connectivity of RNNs, parameterized by rank and sparsity, influences robustness in closed-loop control. We show that low-rank, sparse connectivity induces desirable network dynamics, enabling closed-form continuous-time networks (CfCs) with fewer parameters to outperform their full-rank counterparts under distribution shift.

A Joint Learning Approach for Semi-supervised Neural Topic Modeling
Jeffrey Chiu*, Rajat Mittal*, Neehal Tumma*, Abhishek Sharma, Finale Doshi-Velez
ACL SPNLP, 2022
paper

We introduce the Label-Indexed Neural Topic Model (LI-NTM), the first effective upstream semi-supervised neural topic model. LI-NTM outperforms existing neural topic models in document reconstruction, with the strongest results in low labeled data regimes.
